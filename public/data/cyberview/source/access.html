Data CyberView would like to use

A live, verified picture of cyber threats worldwide for defenders: what happened, what is being exploited, and the outages around it, updated around the clock.

These are the power grid and cyber security sources CyberView would like to show but does not have permission or access to use, the contact each operator publishes, and the request written for each one, in the order to ask.

Why CyberView asks

CyberView only uses a source when the operator's terms of use and its robots.txt allow it, and it never works around a block: no disguised browsers, no solving challenges or captchas, no borrowed keys. Many power companies publish live outage maps whose data is public to look at but whose terms forbid reuse or automated reading. CyberView respects that and asks instead.

Some cyber security sources are listed too: national CERTs and news sites whose feeds CyberView read until their sites began turning automated readers away or their robots.txt asked readers to stay away.

A source is listed here when its terms forbid or restrict reuse or automated access (or could not be found), its robots.txt disallows the data, its site blocks automated readers, it needs a key or an account, or it could not be reached.

What CyberView would do with the data

  • Show current outages and grid alerts as context for defenders, next to cyber events, always labelled as context and never as a claim about their cause. An outage is never called an attack unless an official source says so.
  • Credit the operator by name and link to the operator's own outage map or page.
  • Check for updates every few minutes, no more often, from one identified collector.
  • Collect no personal data: counts, areas and times only.
  • Make no commercial use of it. CyberView is free, non-commercial and open source.

When a source is granted, its entry here changes to Granted and CyberView's builder connects it on those terms. If an operator declines, the entry says so and CyberView leaves the source alone.

How this list is made

CyberView AI (a Claude model) compiled this list on 19 November 2026 from each operator's published terms of use, its robots.txt and one test request per source; the quotations are the operators' own words. On 20 November 2026 it added the contact each operator publishes (read on the operator's own site, respecting its robots.txt; an address is never guessed, and where none could be read the entry says why), wrote each request and set the order to ask in. The list is kept in the public repository. If anything here is wrong about your organisation, or you would like to grant or decline a request, please tell us and we will update it.
